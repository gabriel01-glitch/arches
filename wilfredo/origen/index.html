Wilfredo, where are we from?

You think humans are not from Earth. Tonight we do not laugh at that. We open both folders, then you give the verdict in English.

Do not mix three different questions

Smart people lose this argument because they fight three fights at once. Separate them, and you suddenly sound like a scientist.

En simple: Una cosa es “la vida llegó en una roca”. Otra es “alguien nos trajo a los humanos”. Y otra es “evolucionamos aquí”. No son la misma idea.
Science takes this seriously

1. We evolved here

Life started on Earth (or arrived as chemistry), then changed, slowly, into us. Bones, DNA, and our bodies all point to Africa, then the whole planet.

Possible. Not proven.

2. Panspermia: life hitchhiked

Maybe the first spark of life did not start on Earth. Amino acids ride inside meteorites. A microbe could, in theory, survive a trip on a rock. That would still mean humans evolved here after the spark arrived.

A story, so far

3. Someone dropped humans here

Ancient astronauts, seeders, Anunnaki. This is the claim you are really making. It is the hardest one to prove, and the one with the weakest evidence.

Say this

Those are three different claims. They are not the same thing.

If microbes arrived on a meteorite, and then became humans on Earth, which claim is that?
Yes. The spark can come from the sky. The people still grow up on this planet.
Why does a plumber care about this split?
Exactly. Isolate the claim, then pressure-test it. Same job, bigger scale.

Exhibit B. The family recipe

Our closest living relatives are chimpanzees and bonobos. Non-African people still carry about 1 to 2 percent Neanderthal DNA. We did not only look similar. We mixed.

Si alguien te “sembró” como especie lista, ¿por qué tu receta coincide con la de África y con neandertales que ya estaban en la Tierra?
The other sky answers: “A seeder could use local DNA.” Possible in a story. We have never found a second recipe.

Exhibit C. The soldered pipe

Great apes have 24 pairs of chromosomes. Humans have 23. Two ape chromosomes fused, end to end. The old caps are still in the middle. One old centromere is still there, retired.

Ape 2B

Like two copper pipes soldered into one. The joint is still visible.

Say this

Human chromosome two is a fusion. You can still see the joint.

This is one of the hardest exhibits for the “dropped humans” claim. A seeder would have to fake an ape joint inside every one of us.

Exhibit D. Built for this planet

Vitamin D from this sun. Bones and blood pressure for 1G. Lungs for about 21 percent oxygen. A body clock that expects a 24-hour day. If we were built for another world, this one would fight us harder.

The other sky answers: “They could have designed us for Earth on purpose.” Then we are back to a story that needs a craft, a lab, or a second code. We do not have those.

The sky's folder: some of this is real

If you want to defend your idea like an adult, start here. Do not start with pyramids.

En simple: Lo más fuerte de “no somos de aquí” no son los aliens de televisión. Es la química que cae del cielo.

Exhibit E. Rocks that carry the ingredients

The Murchison meteorite fell in Australia in 1969. Inside: amino acids and nucleobases, the pieces of proteins and DNA. Some of those amino acids are rare on Earth, so this is not just dirt from a farm.

Life’s bricks fall from the sky. That part is not a theory. It is a lab result.

Earth answers: “Bricks are not a house. Amino acids are not a person, and they are not a spaceship.”

Exhibit F. Hitchhikers can survive

Tardigrades survive vacuum and radiation. Rocks blasted off Mars can land on Earth. So a microbe taking a ride is physically possible. Scientists call this panspermia. They argue about it in papers, not in memes.

Say this

Panspermia is possible. It is not the same as ancient astronauts.

Earth answers: “Possible is not proven. And a microbe still has to become Lucy, then you, on this planet.”

Exhibit G. A Nobel winner asked the question

Francis Crick helped discover the shape of DNA. In 1973 he and Leslie Orgel published “Directed Panspermia”: maybe a civilization sent microbes on purpose. They offered a hypothesis, not a proof. Smart people are allowed to ask wild questions. Asking is not answering.

Earth answers: “Crick also said we should look for evidence. Fifty years later, we still have Earth DNA, Earth bones, Earth joints.”

Exhibit H. The ancient astronaut story

Erich von Däniken, 1968, Chariots of the Gods: pyramids, Nazca, “too advanced for humans.” Archaeologists answer with quarries, copper tools, worker villages, and unfinished stones. Zecharia Sitchin’s Anunnaki translations are rejected by Sumerian scholars.

A mystery is not an alien. A mystery is a job.

Las pirámides son impresionantes. Impresionante no es la misma palabra que extraterrestre.
Earth answers: “If this is your best exhibit, the case is leaking. Impressive stonework is what humans do when they have time, food, and a reason.”

En simple: Una afirmación extraordinaria necesita una prueba extraordinaria. “Siento que no somos de aquí” no cierra la válvula.

Phrases for a serious argument

According to the evidence... Según la evidencia... Use this when you have a fact, not a feeling.
On the other hand... Por otro lado... The adult way to change folders.
That is possible, but it is not proven. The golden sentence of this class. Panspermia lives here.
I'm not convinced yet. No “you're stupid.” Just: show me the joint in the pipe.
